# Finishing, Measurement and Packing

## Pressing and finishing sequence

Garments move from sewing to finishing in the order: thread trimming, pressing,
measurement check, final quality audit, metal detection, folding, polybagging
and cartoning. Each stage has a fixed capacity that is independent of the
sewing line, and finishing is a common hidden bottleneck.

Finishing throughput is approximately 1,400 knit garments or 900 woven garments
per day. A sewing line producing 1,530 t-shirts a day will therefore queue at
finishing, and the effective output of the order is set by finishing, not by
sewing.

## Measurement checks

Measurement is checked on a minimum of 15 garments per size per lot, across the
full size range. Points of measure follow the buyer's size specification.
Tolerances are plus or minus 1 cm on chest and length, plus or minus 0.5 cm on
sleeve and shoulder, and plus or minus 0.3 cm on collar.

A size that fails measurement on more than 2 of the 15 checked garments fails
the whole size, and every garment in that size is re-measured.

## Packing ratios and cartons

Unless the buyer specifies otherwise, garments are packed in a standard size
ratio of S:M:L:XL = 1:2:2:1 per carton, 24 units per carton for knits and 20
for woven or fleece. Carton weight must not exceed 15 kg.

## Shipping documentation hold

A carton cannot be released to the shipping area until the packing list, the
inspection certificate and the care label compliance record are attached. An
order that is physically complete but missing documentation is still not
shippable, and this delay is invisible on the production dashboard. Allow half
a day between the last carton packed and the shipment being collection-ready.
