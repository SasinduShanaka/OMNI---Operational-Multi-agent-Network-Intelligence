# Defect Classification and Rework Rules

## Defect classes

Every defect found at inline or final inspection is recorded in one of three
classes. The class determines whether the lot can ship.

Critical defects make the garment unsafe or unsaleable and carry zero
tolerance. These include a broken needle fragment detected in the garment, any
sharp object, a missing or incorrect care label required by law, and wrong
fibre composition on the label.

Major defects would cause a customer to reject or return the garment: broken or
skipped stitching in a seam under stress, a measurement outside tolerance, a
shade mismatch between panels of the same garment, an open seam, a missing
button or a misaligned placket.

Minor defects are visible but would not usually cause a return: a loose thread
end, a slightly uneven topstitch, a light surface mark that will wash out.

## Common defects by construction

Polo and knit collar styles most often fail on collar and placket alignment and
on shade variation between the body and the rib. Formal shirts fail most often
on collar point symmetry and on sleeve placket construction. Fleece hoodies
fail on drawcord length and on hood symmetry. Denim fails on leg twist and on
indigo shade banding across the spread.

## Rework rules

Minor defects are reworked on the line without stopping it. Major defects stop
the affected operation until the root cause is identified, because a major
defect found once in a sample is usually present across a batch.

Any garment reworked twice for the same defect is downgraded to seconds and
removed from the order quantity. This matters for feasibility: a run with a
5 percent rework rate delivers 5 percent fewer units than the line output
suggests, so the planned quantity must include the expected rework allowance.

## Metal detection

Every garment passes through metal detection after finishing. A positive
detection removes the garment and triggers a needle audit on the operation that
produced it. Production on that operation stops until the broken needle is
accounted for in full.
