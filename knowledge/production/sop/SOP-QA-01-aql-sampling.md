# AQL Sampling and Final Inspection

## Purpose and scope

This procedure defines how finished garment lots are sampled and judged before
they are released to packing. It applies to every production order on LINE-001
through LINE-004 and to every SKU in the product master.

Inspection uses ISO 2859-1 General Inspection Level II with an Acceptable
Quality Limit of 2.5 for major defects and 4.0 for minor defects. Critical
defects carry an AQL of zero: a single critical defect fails the lot.

## Sample size by lot size

The sample is drawn from cartons already packed, spread across all cartons in
the lot, never from a single carton.

| Lot size (units) | Sample size | Accept (major) | Reject (major) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 501 to 1,200 | 80 | 5 | 6 |
| 1,201 to 3,200 | 125 | 7 | 8 |
| 3,201 to 10,000 | 200 | 10 | 11 |
| 10,001 to 35,000 | 315 | 14 | 15 |

A 4,000 unit order therefore requires a 200 piece sample and is accepted at up
to 10 major defects. An order of 10,000 units moves into the 315 piece band.

## Inspection timing

Final inspection begins only when at least 80 percent of the lot is packed.
Inspecting earlier produces a sample that is not representative because the
early cartons come from the line's learning curve.

Allow one full working day for final inspection of lots above 3,200 units and
half a day below that. This time sits after the last sewing day and must be
included when a delivery date is promised.

## Failed lots

A rejected lot is returned to the line for 100 percent re-inspection and
rework. Re-inspection of a failed lot requires a fresh sample at the next
stricter level, and the lot cannot be re-offered on the same day it failed.

Plan a minimum of two additional working days whenever a lot fails. Where the
order is already at risk on capacity, a failed lot should be escalated to the
merchandiser before rework begins, because a revised delivery date is usually
cheaper than overtime rework.
