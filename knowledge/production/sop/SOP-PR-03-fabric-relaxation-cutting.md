# Fabric Relaxation, Spreading and Cutting

## Relaxation before spreading

Fabric arriving on rolls is under tension from finishing and winding. Cutting
it before that tension is released produces panels that shrink after sewing and
fail measurement checks at final inspection.

Every roll must be opened, laid flat and relaxed before spreading. Relaxation
time depends on the fabric:

| Fabric | Relaxation time | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| Cotton jersey and pique | 24 hours | GAR-001, GAR-002 |
| Cotton poplin and shirting | 12 hours | GAR-003, GAR-008 |
| Fleece | 24 hours | GAR-004 |
| Rayon and viscose | 24 hours | GAR-005 |
| Denim | 48 hours | GAR-006 |
| Polyester and performance knits | 6 hours | GAR-007 |

Relaxation happens before the line can start. A cutting plan that ignores it
will slip by a full day, and for denim by two days.

## Spreading and shrinkage allowance

Spread tension must be neutral. Cotton knits are cut with a 3 percent shrinkage
allowance, fleece with 4 percent, denim with 5 percent after one wash, and
polyester with 1 percent. Rayon requires 4 percent and must never be spread
more than 60 plies high because the lower plies distort.

## Cutting tolerance and yield

Panel measurements are accepted within plus or minus 0.5 cm for body panels and
plus or minus 0.3 cm for collars, cuffs and plackets. Cutting yield below 92
percent must be reported: it usually indicates a marker problem rather than an
operator problem, and re-making the marker recovers more fabric than re-cutting.

## Bundle release to the line

Cut panels are numbered, bundled by size and released to the line in size
sequence. A line cannot begin sewing on a partial bundle set. Where fabric
arrives late, the cutting room releases the largest size first because those
panels take longest to sew.
