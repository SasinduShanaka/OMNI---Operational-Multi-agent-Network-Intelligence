# Seasonal Demand Calendar — Export and Local

## Export shipping windows

Export demand is shaped by the retail calendar in the destination market rather
than by local seasons. The windows that move volume are:

Northern hemisphere spring and summer programmes ship February to April.
Back-to-school and autumn programmes ship July to September. Holiday and
year-end programmes ship September to November, and this is the single largest
window of the year across all accounts.

January and February are the quietest export months. A dip in a January
forecast is a calendar effect and recurs every year.

## Local and regional demand

Regional demand follows festival and school calendars. The April new year
period lifts casual wear and t-shirts, particularly GAR-002 and GAR-005, in the
four weeks before the holiday. The December season lifts formal shirts and
gifting categories.

Factory output also falls during the April holiday week itself, so a capacity
plan that assumes a full April is optimistic by roughly five working days.

## Category seasonality summary

| Category | Peak months | Trough months |
|---|---|---|
| Polo and t-shirt basics | May to June, October to November | January to February |
| Formal shirts | August to October, December | May to July |
| Fleece and hoodies | June to August placement, ships September to November | January to March |
| Sportswear | Steady year round, mild lift in March and September | None pronounced |
| Denim | September to November | February to April |

## Reading a forecast against this calendar

A trend line fitted to twelve months of history carries these seasonal
movements inside it. Where a forecast increase or decrease lines up with a
window in this calendar, the movement is expected and the recommendation should
say so rather than flagging it as a change in the underlying trend.
